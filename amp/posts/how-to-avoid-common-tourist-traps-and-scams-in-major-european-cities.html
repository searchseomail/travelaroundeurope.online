In Paris, watch out for the 'friendship bracelet' scam near Sacré-Cœur. Men will aggressively tie a string around your wrist and then demand money. Just keep your hands in your pockets and walk right past them without making eye contact.

Rome has the classic 'rose sellers' or guys handing out rosemary branches near the Colosseum. They pretend it's a gift for your girlfriend, but the second you take it, they hound you for euros. A firm 'no gracias' or 'no grazie' works wonders.

Barcelona is notorious for pickpockets, especially around Las Ramblas and the Gothic Quarter. Never leave your phone sitting on a restaurant table, and definitely invest in a money belt or a cross-body bag that zips securely.

One huge tip for restaurants in tourist hubs: if the menu has pictures of the food, photos of the staff, or someone standing outside trying to lure you in, walk away. Locals go to places packed with other locals where the menu is only in the native language.

Beware of the 'found a gold ring' scam on bridges in Paris. Someone taps you on the shoulder, holds up a shiny ring on the ground, and asks if you dropped it. Once you say no, they insist it's your lucky day and demand a finder's fee.

Another classic in Rome is people pretending to work for the Vatican museums or Colosseum offering 'skip-the-line' tickets outside the actual gates. Always book your tickets in advance through the official government websites to avoid getting scammed or ripped off.

In Barcelona, watch out for the 'stain' scam. Someone 'accidentally' spills mustard or ketchup on your jacket, and a helpful stranger rushes over to help clean it up while their accomplice deftly lifts your wallet from your open bag.

Never play the shell game or three-card monte on the streets of Paris or Rome. It looks like people are winning easily, but those are all plants working with the scammer. You will lose your money instantly.

When taking taxis, make sure the meter is actually running before you pull away. In major tourist cities, some unscrupulous drivers will try to claim the meter is broken and quote you a flat, wildly inflated rate at the end of the ride.
