Tipping culture in Europe: A complete country-by-country guide

lost_in_syntax
Topic Starter
Posted: 21.01.2025 09:20
I am planning a multi-country backpacking trip across Europe this summer and I am stressing out about the different tipping customs everywhere I go. I know the US has a very strict 20% rule, but I have heard it ranges from completely unnecessary to slightly offensive in parts of Europe. Could someone break down the actual tipping etiquette for countries like France, Italy, Germany, and Spain so I don't accidentally insult anyone or overspend?

static_dreamer
Replied: 27.02.2025 05:02
Don't stress at all! Tipping in Europe is nothing like the US. Service charges are almost always included in the bill, so anything extra is just a nice bonus for exceptional service, never an obligation.
lunar_dust
Replied: 13.03.2025 01:27
In France, service is legally included ('service compris'). You don't need to leave 20%. Just rounding up the bill or leaving a couple of euros in cash is more than enough if you had a good meal.
circuit_soul
Replied: 03.04.2025 15:06
Italy is super relaxed about this. Tipping is not expected at all in most restaurants. Sometimes you'll see a 'coperto' (cover charge) on the bill, which is a per-person fee for the table and bread, but that goes to the restaurant, not the server.
shadow_play
Replied: 10.05.2025 05:14
For Germany, rounding up to the nearest euro or adding about 5 to 10 percent is standard practice, but only if you pay in cash. If you just hand over your card, it's harder to tip, so keep some small bills handy.
silent_echoes
Replied: 29.06.2025 11:23
Spain is probably the most casual. Locals usually just leave the small loose change (like 20 to 50 cents) from paying in cash, or maybe a single euro coin if the service was really great. Leaving 20% would definitely shock them!

A common mistake Americans make is trying to leave a tip on their credit card reader. Most European servers prefer cash tips left on the table because digital tips don't always reach the actual staff due to tax and labor laws.

Honestly, just breathe! No one is going to yell at you or chase you down the street for undertipping. European wages and benefits for hospitality workers are vastly different from the US tipping-dependent system.
