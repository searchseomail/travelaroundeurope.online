Everything you need to know about traveling to Europe with a pet

prism_drift
Topic Starter
Posted: 10.01.2025 02:11
Planning a big move and a multi-week vacation across several EU countries with my golden retriever next spring. I am totally overwhelmed by the microchip requirements, the EU animal health certificate timelines, and whether airlines require cargo or allow in-cabin based on size. Could anyone who has successfully navigated this process recently share their checklist and top tips for a smooth trip?

First off, take a deep breath! A golden retriever will definitely have to travel in the cargo hold unless they are a certified service animal, because they are way too big for in-cabin rules (which usually cap the pet plus carrier weight at around 15-20 lbs).
lost_in_syntax
Replied: 10.02.2025 07:16
The microchip rule is strict: it MUST be implanted BEFORE the rabies vaccination, or the vaccine doesn't count according to EU regulations. Double-check your vet records right now to ensure the dates match up properly.
ever_green
Replied: 04.06.2025 14:32
For the timeline, your EU animal health certificate has to be issued by a USDA-accredited veterinarian (if you're coming from the US) and then endorsed within 10 days of entering the EU. Timing is everything here, so book your USDA endorsement appointment ahead of time.

When you are planning your itinerary, remember that traveling across borders with a dog is actually pretty seamless once you are in the EU zone, but you might want to look into Backpacking Europe: What are YOUR Top 10 Essential Tips for First-Timers? to get some great general transit advice that also applies to managing luggage and a dog.
pixel_voyager
Replied: 16.08.2025 05:33
Airline crates need to be IATA-compliant. Get your golden retriever used to the crate months in advance with lots of positive reinforcement. Put a familiar-smelling blanket inside so they feel secure during the flight.
gilded_path
Replied: 09.10.2025 02:21
Don't forget the tapeworm treatment (echinococcus multilocularis) if you are entering certain countries like Ireland, Finland, Malta, or Norway. It must be administered by a vet between 24 and 120 hours before entering those specific countries and officially logged in the health certificate.
ever_green
Replied: 11.10.2025 11:48
If you want to mix in some relaxed days or need slower-paced travel days to let your dog decompress from the journey, you might find inspiration in Senior travel in Europe: Best accessible tours and slow-paced itineraries which highlights great low-stress routes.
star_bound
Replied: 22.11.2025 05:08
Also, make sure you download the Pet Travel app or check the USDA APHIS website religiously. Rules change slightly depending on whether you are coming from a rabies-controlled or rabies-free designated country.
amber_drift
Replied: 23.11.2025 13:16
Train travel in Europe with a large dog is surprisingly easy in countries like France, Germany, and Italy—most require a ticket for the dog, a muzzle for larger breeds, and a leash, but they are generally very welcome on high-speed trains!
